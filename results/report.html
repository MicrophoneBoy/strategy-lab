Strategy Lab report

Does any simple, well-known trading idea beat buying and holding SPY, after costs and taxes, by more than luck? Nothing here beat simply holding SPY in a way that could be told apart from luck. The answer so far is: buy the index fund.

9ideas tested
154versions tried
0survivors

How an idea is judged

  1. Beat SPY on the first 4 years. The best version must earn more per year than buy-and-hold SPY, after costs and an estimated tax.
  2. Be better than luck. The price history is shuffled thousands of times, which keeps every day's move but destroys any real pattern. The idea is re-optimised on each shuffled copy. If shuffled data does as well as real data, the result is luck.
  3. Beat SPY out of sample. Pick settings on the past 4 years, trade the next month, roll forward.
  4. Be better than luck out of sample. The shuffle test again, on the walk-forward run.

An idea is dead at the first gate it fails, and gates 3 and 4 only run for ideas that pass 1 and 2. Every idea tested makes a lucky survivor more likely, so the bar for the shuffle tests is 0.01 divided by the number of ideas tested so far. The most recent 12 months of data are locked away for a final exam that only a survivor may take.

1. Moving-average crossover

Dead at gate 1, gate 2. In its first 4 years (1993 to 1997) the best version trailed SPY by 1.94% a year. 463 of 1,000 shuffled price histories did at least as well (p = 0.4635, needed below 0.0100), so even that result looks like luck.

Rule: Hold SPY while its fast moving average is above its slow one, otherwise hold cash.
Why it might work, and who loses: Trend following: prices that have been rising keep rising for a while because investors react slowly to news. The loser is whoever sells early in a trend.
Tested on: SPY, first 4 years 1993-02-01 to 1997-01-23. Versions tried: 90. Best version: fast 35, slow 60.

2. DTFM (dual-timeframe momentum)

Dead at gate 2. In its first 4 years (1999 to 2003) the best version beat SPY by 5.78% a year, but 843 of 1,000 shuffled price histories did at least as well (p = 0.8432, needed below 0.0050), so that looks like luck.

Rule: Hold up to 3 assets whose 20-session and 60-session returns are both positive, 10% of the account each, reviewed weekly, with an 8% trailing stop. Rules fixed in advance.
Why it might work, and who loses: Dual-timeframe momentum: assets rising over both 20 and 60 sessions tend to keep rising, as investors are slow to price in good news, and a trailing stop cuts losers. The loser is whoever sells a winner early.
Tested on: QQQ plus 9 sector ETFs, first 4 years 1999-03-11 to 2003-03-13. Versions tried: 1. Best version: buy_weight 0.1, fast 20, max_weight 0.15, slots 3, slow 60, stop 0.08.

Assumptions and limits

  • Daily bars from yahoo, adjusted for splits and dividends, up to 2025-11-13. Costs: 0 bps fee plus 2 bps slippage on whatever changes hands. Tax estimate: 22% on gains held a year or less, 15% on longer holds. Cash earns 0. Trades happen at the close that produced the signal.
  • Each verdict rests on one 4-year window. Ideas that beat SPY did so in windows where SPY fell or lagged, and the shuffle test showed no timing skill behind it.
  • The shuffle test credits timing only. It keeps each asset's total return, so an asset that simply did better earns the idea nothing.
  • Past results say nothing certain about the future. This is a research project, not advice.
